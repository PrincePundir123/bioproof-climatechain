StubbleTrail connects agricultural residue to an inspectable evidence trail, from farmer declaration to independent review. The question behind this project is simple: when a sustainability claim is made, can we examine the records supporting it?

Consider this synthetic example. A collector records six hundred kilograms, but the processor receives five hundred and sixty. Without connected records, the difference is difficult to investigate. A receipt might also be reused across separate claims. StubbleTrail brings measurements, documents and accountable decisions together, helping reviewers investigate discrepancies before relying on a supply-chain claim. These examples demonstrate the prototype; they are not field-pilot results.

The workflow has four distinct roles. A farmer registers a plot and declares available residue. A collector records pickup quantity, measurement basis and event time. A processor independently records intake, the facility and receipt details. Supporting documents are attached to the batch, and an auditor reviews the evidence and findings before recording a decision. In the operational application, permissions are checked on the server; the role selector shown here belongs only to the isolated demo.

AI assists with document reading. The Gemini integration has been verified using a synthetic PDF, extracting its receipt number and weight correctly. In the application, the uploader can request structured fields, compare each suggestion with the original document and correct uncertain values. Confirmation appends a review event; it does not silently replace a custody measurement or approve the batch. Original files remain private, and extraction requests are subject to database-enforced usage limits. Human accountability remains part of the process.

StubbleTrail also looks across claims. Here, two synthetic batches share a receipt identifier and identical file bytes. The graph makes that relationship visible, while the reviewer documents an explanation. A match is an investigation signal, not proof of fraud. SoilSafe adds another question: how much residue is proposed for removal? It keeps soil cover, nutrients and site-specific review in view, without claiming a universal safe removal threshold.

Each event is linked to the previous event through a canonical SHA-256 hash chain. Let me demonstrate the integrity check: the recorded chain verifies, but changing a quantity in this copied event produces a mismatch. The original record remains unchanged. This makes alterations detectable against the recorded history. It is not a guarantee against a database administrator rewriting an unanchored chain, which is why the project also supports external commitments on Ethereum Sepolia.

The public portal presents a redacted record, custody summary and verification result without exposing private source files or identities. The Solidity registry is deployed on Sepolia with a verified signing authority. Approved real records can commit their approved event root to that contract. A confirmed status requires both a successful transaction and a matching registry proof. This synthetic sample intentionally shows no confirmed anchor. Blockchain helps verify record integrity; it cannot prove a physical delivery occurred.

The prototype is deployed, with Supabase authentication and private storage, Gemini extraction, and the Sepolia registry. Ninety-three automated unit, API and database tests pass, alongside desktop and mobile workflow tests and local contract checks. The intended users are cooperatives, residue aggregators and independent reviewers. Next steps include hosted acceptance testing, organization-scoped access, scalable conflict queries and a documented field pilot.
